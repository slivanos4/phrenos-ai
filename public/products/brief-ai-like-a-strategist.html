The idea in one line

AI does not need clever wording. It needs a clear brief: outcome, audience, constraints, and what “good” looks like.

Before you type anything

Answer these four questions (PAST):

QuestionExample
PurposeWhat decision or deliverable do you need?“A one-page options memo for the leadership team”
AudienceWho will use or judge the output?“CFO and COO; sceptical of hype”
ScopeWhat is in and out?“UK retail ops only; no marketing use cases”
ToneHow should it sound?“Direct, board-ready, British English”

If you cannot answer PAST, you are not ready to brief the model. You are still clarifying the work.

The briefing template (copy and reuse)


Role: Act as a [specific expert] advising [who].



Objective: Produce [exact deliverable] so that [named person] can [decision or action].



Context:

- Organisation / situation: […]

- What we already know: […]

- What must not be assumed: […]



Constraints:

- Include: […]

- Exclude: […]

- Length / format: […]

- Tone: […]

- Language: British English



Definition of done:

- Must contain: […]

- Must avoid: […]

- Success looks like: […]



Sources / inputs (if any):

- [paste facts, notes, or say “use only what I provide”]

Five upgrades that change the quality

1. Name the decision.

Weak: “Tell me about AI for customer service.”

Strong: “Recommend whether we pilot an AI assistant for Tier-1 support this quarter. Give a yes/no recommendation and three risks.”

2. Force structure.

Ask for headings, tables, or a fixed outline. Ambiguous asks produce essay sludge.

3. Separate inventing from analysing.

If the facts matter, paste them and say: “Use only the information below. If something is missing, list questions. Do not invent numbers.”

4. Demand options, not one answer.

“Give three options with trade-offs, then your recommendation.” Leaders need choice architecture, not a single confident paragraph.

5. Add a review pass.

After the first draft: “Challenge this as a sceptical CFO. What is weak, missing, or overclaimed?”

Prompt patterns that earn their keep

Strategy memo

“Draft a one-page memo for [audience]. Situation, complication, question, recommendation, risks, next step. Max 400 words.”

Process redesign

“Map the current workflow for [process] in steps. Mark where judgement is required. Propose where AI assists vs where a human must remain accountable.”

Risk scan

“List failure modes for [use case]. For each: likelihood (H/M/L), impact (H/M/L), early warning, owner role.”

Meeting prep

“I have a 30-minute meeting with [role] about [topic]. Give: 5 questions I should ask, 3 traps to avoid, 1 crisp opening line.”

Output quality check

“Score the draft below 1–5 on clarity, evidence, actionability, and risk honesty. Then rewrite only what fails.”

Red flags (your briefing is broken)

  • You ask for “ideas” with no decision attached
  • You accept the first polished answer without a challenge pass
  • The model invents metrics and you keep them
  • Nobody is named as the human accountable for the result
  • The output could belong to any company in any industry

    Remember

    Prompt engineering for professionals is not wordplay. It is work briefing: clear outcomes, honest constraints, and human judgement at the end.

    When the brief is sharp, almost any capable model looks smarter.

    When the brief is vague, no model will save you.
